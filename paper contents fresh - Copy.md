**Privacy-Preserving Federated Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Scalable Epidemic Control (PF-MARL)**



III. Methodology

A. System Architecture Overview



The PF-MARL framework is architected as a three-tier hierarchical system to perform collaborative epidemic intelligence analysis under strong data sovereignty. As illustrated in Fig. \[X], the architecture is structurally divided into three layers:



1\. Distributed Edge Clients Layer



The Distributed Edge Clients Layer serves as the foundational tier of the architecture that comprises N heterogeneous Edge Clients that represents  different urban administrative districts. Each individual client is a data-sovereign, simulating local epidemic spread and implementing independent control strategies.



1\) Localized Urban Simulation Environment: Each district has its localized simulation environment, relying upon a SEIR (Susceptible Exposed Infected Recovered) mathematical framework. This component models the stochastic process of pathogen transmission in the district. A key element in this environment is the Mobility Inter-connectivity, which represents how people move between different neighbouring districts thus allowing infection to cross these borders.

2\) Local Actor  π₍θ₎ : The Local Actor is the one executing the policy-making logic of each district. This agent is by no means monolithic; rather, it is organized as a cooperative hierarchy of four specialized sub-agents, which balance independent control over specific vector(s) of the urban response:



Citizen (Behavior): Governs individual compliance with social distancing and protective measures.



Hospital (Testing): Orchestrates allocation of medical resources and level of diagnostic testing.



Policy (Lockdown): Regulates the severity of commercial restrictions and gathering bans.



Transport (Mobility): Handles transit "valves" that govern inter-district mobility weights.

3)Closed-Loop Experience Generation:

 All Edge Client functions work within an ongoing feedback loop. The Local Actor gets a slice of State Observation which includes Current Infections, Population and Economy metrics. In response, it produces policy action consisting of Lockdown Levels and Health Measures. When running, the environment computes a Dual-Objective Reward (a penalizing function with two weighted tasks, virus suppression and economic preservation). All of this transition is recorded as Store Experience and logged within Local Replay Buffer, which will serve necessary data for the next pseudo-private training phase.



2\. Multi-Agent RL \& Differential Privacy Layer

The Multi-Agent RL\&DPL layer is the system's "global intelligence", processing the collective experiences of all districts, under strong privacy constraints. This layer implements a Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) framework, designed to optimize the global pandemic response without exposing raw sensitive data.

1\) Centralized critic Q₍φ₎ : It is the thing holds the whole learning process together. This step collects Joint Experiences (states, actions, and rewards) from all Local Replay Buffers of N Edge Clients. The Critic observes the entire city state at once and evaluates the combined effect of all district-level policies. This macroscopic perspective is needed to capture the holistic, delayed and cross boundary effects of mobility and infection spread that individual districts cannot see when considering only placental data.

2\) DP Engine (Opacus) \& Gradient Scrubbing: In addition, we add a DP engine (Opacus), in an attempt to prevent the Critic from "memorizing" individual medical records or sensitive district patterns. Gradient Scrubbing acts on the gradients generated by Centralized Critic when it backpropagates. This involves two mathematically intense steps:



Gradient Clipping: To bound the influence of a single experience each per-sample gradient is clipped to a fixed max norm (C).

Noise Addition: The clipped gradients are supplemented with calibrated Gaussian noise proportionate to the function's sensitivity. An adversary cannot reconstruct the original "Joint Experiences" from the model parameters thanks to this dual-process guarantee that the resulting model updates satisfy (ϵ,δ)

-Differential Privacy.

3\) Hook-Free Critic Clone and Causal Loop: The noise injection induces instability to the policy gradients, which is a key challenge for DP-MARL. To prevent this, we implement a Weight Synchronization mechanism to uphold a Hook-Free Critic Clone. This clone is a "clean" structural version of the Centralized Critic and gets updated with core weights but is not hooked to the same DP-process. This clone is used exclusively to generate the Policy Feedback Signals (Actor gradients). These signals are transmitted back down to the Local Actors (Layer 1), providing a stable and private mathematical "instruction" on how to refine local lockdown and health measures to achieve a city-wide Pareto-optimal result.



3\. Federated Learning \& Global Convergence Layer

The Federated Learning \& Global Convergence Layer forms the tip of architecture, enabling individual district models to converge into a unified urban pandemic strategy. The Federated Learning Hub (Server), which is the physical host of this layer, maintains the lifecycle of a global model without ever seeing the local raw data.



1\) Global Critic Aggregator: At the heart of this layer is the Global Critic Aggregator. Its task is to gather the Anonymized Model Gradients sent up by every Edge Client. Its task is to gather the Anonymized Model Gradients sent up by every Edge Client(via the DP Engine from Layer 2). Because these gradients have already been "scrubbed" for privacy at the edge, the Hub can safely process them without risking the re-identification of individual health records or specific district-level outbreaks.

2\) Federated Averaging (FedAvg):

To enable the model to converge effectively across the distributed urban network, the Aggregator employs the FedAvg algorithm.

2\) Mobility Inter-connectivity

 Edges E are weighed by a basic mobility factor, wij \[0. 4, 1. 0]which stand for the transit ability between two districts, i and j. The produced Mobility Matrix M row-normalized to keep the stability is:

$$M\_{ij} = \\frac{w\_{ij}}{\\sum\_{j=1}^N w\_{ij}}$$ This matrix defines the "Infection Pressure" that spreads the pathogen across the city graph.



2.Stochastic SEIR-M Disease Dynamics

 The biological process of an epidemic is modeled as a discrete-time stochastic SEIR-M (Susceptible-Exposed-Infected-Recovered-Mobility) structure.

1\) State Transition Equations

 At every time unit $t$, the compartments of the population for each district $i$ get updated as shown below:

S to E (Infection): The count of new exposed people is a result of the local effective transmission rate combined with the global infection pressure: $$E\_{i, t+1} = \\beta\_{eff} \\cdot S\_{i, t} \\cdot \\left( \\sum\_{j=1}^N M\_{ij} \\cdot I\_{j, t} \\right) + \\epsilon$$ where $\\epsilon \\sim \\mathcal{N}(0, \\sigma^2)$ represents environmental stochasticity.



E to I (Incubation): The movement between these states depends on the incubation rate $\\sigma$: $$I\_{i, t+1} = \\sigma \\cdot E\_{i, t}$$

I to R (Recovery): Recovery is regulated by the parameter $\\gamma$: $$R\_{i, t+1} = \\gamma \\cdot I\_{i, t}$$

2\) Policy Attenuation

The Effective Transmission Rate ($\\beta\_{eff}$) is not a fixed number. It changes over time through the Health Measures ($h\_i$) given by the Local Actor: $$\\beta\_{eff} = \\beta\_{base} \\cdot (1 - \\phi \\cdot h\_i)$$ where $\\phi = 0. 8$ is the maximum effectiveness of a combined testing and a masking protocol.



3\. Reward and Observation Logic

The environment provides a unique state observation vector for each district consisting of: State Space ($s\_i$): \[Population, Base Mobility, Economic Weight, Current Infection Level].Action Space ($a\_i$): A continuous vector $\[a\_{lock}, a\_{health}] \\in \[0, 1]^2$, where $a\_{lock}$ is a factor that controls the decrease in $M\_{ij}$ while $a\_{health}$ is a factor that controls the attenuation of $\\beta\_{eff}$. The Dual-Objective Reward ($R\_i$) is the signal that instructs the agents to find a balance between maintaining public health and the economy: $$R\_i = - (\\alpha \\cdot I\_{total}^2 + \\beta \\cdot \\text{Economic Cost}i)$$



Economic Cost is measured as a sum of penalties due to mobility restrictions and the cost of health measure deployment: $$\\text{Cost}i = (1 - \\text{Mobility}{i, actual}) + 0.1 \\cdot a{health, i}$$ This exponential penalty on $I\_{total}$ plays a key role in making the agents give the highest priority to "Crushing the Curve" during peak outbreaks.



C. Privacy-Preserving Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

The fundamental computational backbone of the PF-MARL system is the Privacy-Preserving Federated Multi-Agent Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (PF-MADDPG) algorithm. This section provides a formal definition of the training goal, how Differential Privacy (DP) is incorporated,  and the isolation strategy employed to stabilize noisy gradient updates.

1\) Problem Formulation and CTDE: Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) framework, which is a solution to the multi-agent non-stationarity problem, is what we use. Each district $i \\in {1, \\dots, N}$ is governed by a local Actor network $\\pi\_{\\theta\_{i}}$ that maps the local observation $s\_i$ to a continuous action space $a\_i \\in \[0, 1]^k$. To obtain global value function, during the training phase, Centralized Critic ($Q\_{\\phi}$) is used that processes the joint state $S = (s\_1, \\dots, s\_N)$ and the joint action $A = (a\_1, \\dots, a\_N)$. The Critic parameters $\\phi$ are updated by minimizing the Mean Squared Bellman Error (MSBE): $$\\mathcal{L}(\\phi) = \\mathbb{E}{s a r, s'} \[ (Q{\\phi}(S, A) - y)^2 ]$$ where $y = R + \\gamma Q\_{\\phi'}(S', a'\_1, \\dots, a'N) \\mid{a'j = \\pi{\\theta'\_j}(s'\_j)}$ and $\\phi', \\theta'$ are the parameters of the target networks that are slowly tracking.



3\) Weight-Synchronized Hook-Free Isolation: Injecting noise (using $\\tilde{G}$) in the Critic gradients is one of the main reasons that the Actor gradients get corrupted and then the policy updates eventually fail to reach convergence. Besides, we use Hook-Free Critic Clone ($Q\_{\\text{clone}}$) as part of an Isolation Strategy to loosen this burden. - The $Q\_{\\phi}$ (Main Critic) is "hooked" to the DP-engine to ensure that updates are private.



\- The $Q\_{\\text{clone}}$ (Hook-Free Clone) has the same architecture but runs without DP-hooks. - Sometimes, the Main Critic weights $\\phi$ (after the noise has been added) are synchronized to the Clone. The Actor policy gradient is then sourced only from the stable clone: $$\\nabla\_{\\theta\_i} J \\approx \\mathbb{E}{s, a \\sim \\mathcal{D}} \[ \\nabla{\\theta\_i} \\\\pi\_{\\theta\_i}(s\_i) \\nabla\_{a\_i} Q\_{\\text{clone}}(S, A) ]$$ This division guarantees that whereas the Critic furnishes a privately-estimated value signal, the Actors policy update remains differentiable and stable without being directly subjected to per-update Gaussian noise.



D. Federated Learning Protocol and Global Consensus

The final tier of the PF-MARL framework is the Federated Learning Protocol. It makes possible the city-wide coordination of pandemic control measures.This layer  provides the mechanism for "Herd Intelligence, " where each district can get advantages from the management successes and failuress in the urban network on a larger scale.



1\) Decentralized Synchronization Cycle: The whole system is built around a Flower (flwr) framework that the Federated Learning Hub (Server) and the $N$ distributed Edge Clients use to communicate. There are $T$ global communication rounds into which training is divided. Each round $t$, all district clients $k$ complete a fixed number of Local Episodes ($E$). It is at this stage that the PF-MADDPG engine optimizes local parameters based on the district-specific Replay Buffer while the Hook-Free Isolation mechanism ensures stability.



2\) Anonymized Parameter Aggregation: After the local execution phase ends, instead of sending raw health or mobility data, individual districts only share the $(\\epsilon, \\delta)$-private parameters of their local Centralized Critic models. Then, the Global Critic Aggregator at the Hub makes a weighted average of these parameters by means of FedAvg algorithm:

$$\\theta\_{global} \\leftarrow \\sum\_{k=1}^{K} \\frac{n\_{k}}{n} \\theta\_{t}^{k}$$



where $n\_{k}$ represents the number of local training experiences processed by the $k$-th district, $n = \\sum\_{k=1}^{K} n\_{k}$ denotes the total aggregate volume of experiences, and $\\theta\_{t}^{k}$ corresponds to the private, scrubbed model updates from district $k$ at round $t$.



3\) Global Convergence and Model Broadcasting: After aggregating, the Shared Global Model Weights (global) are sent back to the districts involved. This practice guarantees that the overall strategy gradually adjusts to a level that is resistant to local epidemic spikes.



Local Weight Injection: The local Centralized Critic in each district gets replaced by the global weights, which means "restarting" the local value function to align with the city-wide agreement.

Pareto-Optimality: This ongoing sequence of exclusive local adjustment and worldwide federated agreement allows the urban network to onverge toward a high-performance pandemic control policy that is socially optimal and privacy-secure.

IV. Results and Discussion

A. Learning Efficiency and Convergence Analysis

The ability of the system to find the best pandemic management policies even when very strict privacy constraints were in place was measured over a training horizon of 5,000 episodes (Fig. \[X]). This study is an indicator of the technical robustness of the framework when it is subjected to differential privacy perturbations.



1\) Reward Convergence and Policy Acquisition:  The top panel of Fig. \[X] shows the change of the cumulative moving average reward, which is a dual-objective of health preservation and economic stability. The Result: Starting from an initial stochastic state  of -25.0, the Reward gradually converges to its steady-state level of -17.6.



The Meaning: This is a clear indication of how well the Hook-Free Isolation Strategy works. Since the noisy critic updates from the privacy-preserving noise injection are separated from the policy-gradient, the agents are able to learn the strategy of maximizing the utility, which is usually unstable due to privacy-preserving noise injection.



2\) Epidemiological Equilibrium: The bottom panel (Epidemic Control) tracks the systemic infection levels throughout the training period. The result: After the first exploratory phase, the overall infected population in the urban network reduces drastically and stays at a resilient equilibrium of 20 people per time step.



The meaning: This level-off implies the learned coordinated global policy was so effective as to "crush the curve." The absence of any resurgence during the last 3,000 episodes demonstrates the long-term effectiveness of the PF-MARL approach in keeping a low-infection steady state in spite of different district scenarios.



B. Comparative Performance Evaluation

The PF-MARL framework was compared to four other widely used control methods in order to show that it is better: (i) Centralized MADDPG (without privacy constraints), (ii) Static Lockdown (complete closure of the city), (iii) No-Action (free movement), and (iv) FedMADDPG (No-DP) (federated but without differential privacy).



1\) Economic Resilience Analysis: Each strategy's effect on the efficiency of urban economic activities is depicted in the Economic Resilience Index (Fig. \[X]). 

The result:The PF-MARL scheme sustains a level of economic activity that is 52. 6% of the pre-pandemic period. On the other hand, a conventional Total Lockdown policy would shrink economic activity to just 7. 5%.

The Meaning: This 7x improvement in economic resilience demonstrates that the proposed AI-driven policy is capable of keeping over half of the city's business infrastructure operational while still managing the crisis. This validates the "Economic Stability" objective of the research.



2\) Infection Suppression Comparison (Fig. \[Y]): The ability of each strategy to slow down virus transmission was measured mainly by looking at the highest infection numbers during the trials. The Result: The urban network under the No-Action scenario, shows a quick infection peak of 358.22 individuals. With the PF-MARL system, this peak is only 181.04 individuals.



The Meaning: This is a reduction of 49.46% in the highest number of infections than without any control measures. Also, the PF-MARL method gave 32.08% better results in lowering infections than the Random Allocation method (266.50 individuals), demonstrating that the learned collaboration between districts is mathematically a better approach compared to uncoordinated health responses. The negligible 0.05% utility gap between the PF-MARL (Private) and the Centralized MADDPG (Non-Private) models confirms that our system achieves near-optimal pandemic suppression without risking the exposure of sensitive health records.

D. Impact of Differential Privacy (Utility Gap)

The final dimension of this evaluation examines the "Privacy Tax", which refers to how much the system slows down as a result of adding the $(\\epsilon, \\delta)$-Differential Privacy feature.his is quantified by contrasting the private PF-MARL framework with the  FedMADDPG(non-private) baseline.

1\) Quantifying the Efficiency Gap: A comparison of the steady-state indicators shows that there is a noticeable performance gap between the privatized and the non-privatized methodologies. Economic Impact: Under a strict, mathematically guaranteed privacy budget of $\\epsilon = 2.0$ and $\\delta = 10^{-5}$, the non-private model achieves an optimal economic cost of 19.7 units, whereas the private PF-MARL model incurs a cost of 31.1 units. This represents a utility gap of 11.4 units in overall economic efficiency, directly attributable to the gradient perturbations enforced by the $(\\epsilon, \\delta)$-Differential Privacy layer.





Health Aspect: Interestingly, the overall infection rate remains almost steady, even the private model attains a slightly lower average of 160.2 infections whereas the non-private baseline is 167.3.

2\) Examination of the "Privacy Tax": The findings reveal that the major price of incorporating differential privacy is not health safety loss, but the reduction in economic accuracy. The random noise introduced to the gradients makes the district agents take more 'cautious' policy. Due to the fact privacy noise prevents agents from accurately mapping the economic-utility landscape, they end up with health interventions that are more aggressive (and therefore more expensive). Such a large 11.4 units efficiency gap is considered a legitimate "Deployment Tax. " As the non-private baseline involves a centralization of sensitive patient data (which is legally and ethically prohibited), the PF-MARL method is practically the only solution for urban real-world networks. Giving up slightly economic efficient is not a big loss if one in turn gains mathematically data sovereignty over the health city infrastructure.

V. Conclusion and future work 

A. conclusion: In this research, a novel framework for Privacy-Preserving Federated Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (PF-MARL) has been proposed here as a means to solve the critical problem of opposing the preservation of public health and the stability of economy during a pandemic in an urban area. The combination of PF-MADDPG with a powerful Differential Privacy layer and a Federated Learning protocol gave the method the capability of allowing independent districts running the pandemic to react in a joint manner despite the fact that there is no sharing of any sensitive health data at the regions. Based on the experiments, it can be said that the PF-MARL framework does not only come up with a policy which has a high utility but is also a good compromise that is better than the traditional, non-coordinated crisis management approaches. In fact, the framework resulted in peak infections being reduced by up to 49.46% while the economic resilience index was still kept at 52.6%, a level which is almost 7x improvement than that of the total lockdown. Besides this, the proof of a very small utility gap between private and non-private models is a guarantee that mathematical data sovereignty can be realized with the least possible loss of efficiency of interventions. In the final analysis, this paper sets out a scalable base for the use of decentralized, privacy-conscious artificial intelligence in the handling of complex, multi-objective urban crises.
